// SUBNET WORKBENCH — STEP 7 CONCEPTS

Hero Visual Mockups

Two treatments per mode, all on live example data from the tested engine. These are disposable sketches for picking elements, not implementations. Interactions (hover weights, dragging, zoom) are described in captions where a static sketch can't show them.

// CALCULATE

1 · Bit Boundary View

All 32 bits of 192.168.1.0/26, network vs host, with the prefix boundary drawn through them. The single image that makes subnetting click.

1ARibbon — one row, octet gaps

One unbroken left-to-right read: the address is one 32-bit number, octets are just punctuation. Hovering a host bit would show its weight (32, 16, 8...). /31 and /32 render honestly: the amber line just slides to the far right.

1BOctet Stack — four rows, mask alongside

Vertical layout pairs each octet's binary with its decimal and its mask octet, so 255.255.255.192 stops being magic. Better on mobile; weaker at showing "one continuous boundary".

// CALCULATE

2 · Interactive Prefix Slider

Drag /24 → /26 and watch the space split live. Shown here frozen at /26 inside a /24.

2ASplitting Bar — slider over one address bar

The bar animates: dragging right splits each block in half with a quick divide animation, dragging left merges. The dashed ghost lines telegraph what the next notch will do.

2BHalving Tree — ancestry stays visible

Keeps the halving story on screen: a /26 is a /24 cut twice. Costs vertical space and gets deep quickly past /28, so rows would collapse to the last three levels while dragging.

// OVERLAP

3 · Address-Space Map

Subnets positioned along real address space, grouped by nearest common parent (the confirmed scaling answer), collisions highlighted. Example: a branch /25 swallowed by a site /24, and two identical VPN pools.

3ALanes — conflicts drop to a sub-lane under an amber band

The overlapping range itself is the highlighted object — the amber hatch IS the collision, spanning every lane it touches. Warning vs error read as light vs heavy hatch plus the tag. Groups scale independently, so a /25 next to a /8 elsewhere never becomes a subpixel. Click a group header to zoom that group to full width.

3BSpans — one thin row per subnet, dense-paste friendly

Gantt-style: every subnet keeps its own row, so a 40-line customer paste stays legible — conflicts read as vertically aligned amber bars inside a hatched column. Less dramatic than 3A, much better at scale. Hover a row to highlight its partners.

// VLSM

4 · Allocation Diagram

The supernet as one block carved into allocations, stranded space shown explicitly. Live data: 10.0.0.0/24 → Engineering 100, Sales 50, Ops 20, one unlabeled 12.

4ACarved Bar — space is drawn to scale

Proportional truth: Engineering visibly costs half the supernet, and the amber hatch inside each block is the rounding tax you paid for it. Weakness: sub-/28 blocks get too small to label inline and need leader lines, as shown.
